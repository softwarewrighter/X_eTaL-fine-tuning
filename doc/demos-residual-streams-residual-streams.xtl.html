programdemos/residual-streams/residual-streams.xtl

Residual streams: why a deep network needs a skip connection, and what happens when there are four of them. Part 1: a list of 8 numbers sent through 48 small layers (a block 8 -> 16 -> 8 with GELU, its weights fixed by a formula, a different table for every layer), once with each layer replacing the list by the block's output and once with each layer adding the block's output to the list it was given (the residual, or skip, connection); the size of the list after every layer, both ways. Part 2: Hyper-Connections (Xie and others, arXiv:2512.24880): the list widened to S = 4 streams, a 4 x 8 table, and one layer of it as a pipeline, stage by stage: H_pre mixes the streams, the merge averages them, the block runs once on the average, H_post spreads its output back over the streams, H_res mixes the carried state, and the two are added. The mixing tables start from the logits 2 I - 1 (the Python's start); H_res is their exponential (HC) or its Sinkhorn-Knopp projection (mHC: rows and columns each summing to 1). The expressions are written out here (ask FM13 of X_eTaL-ML, docs/xetal-ML-asks.md).

Part 2: four streams, and one layer of Hyper-Connections

ones : Float

value · line 94

The mixing tables start from the logits 2 I - 1: 1 on the diagonal, -1 off it. H_pre and H_post are their row softmax (NN's): each row sums to 1. H_res is their exponential (HC: e on the diagonal, 1/e off it, every row summing to 3.82) or, for mHC, that exponential projected by 20 steps of Sinkhorn-Knopp: every row divided by its sum, then every column by its sum, 20 times over, until rows and columns each sum to 1 (the paper's eq. 9).
