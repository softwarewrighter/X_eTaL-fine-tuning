programdemos/lora-vs-fa/lora-vs-fa.xtl

LoRA beside LoRA-FA, in two experiments kept apart. Experiment A, approximating a known matrix: both fit a known rank-4 shift T from the same seeded A, B at 0; LoRA moves A and B, LoRA-FA moves B alone. The best any update of the rank can do (the Eckart-Young optimum, from T's singular values), and the floor LoRA-FA cannot pass: its update s B A has its rows in the row space of the frozen A, a subspace, so the closest it can come to T is T's projection onto that subspace. Each learned update is projected onto the row space and the remainder measured. Experiment B, learning from examples: the task of lesson 3 (examples made by the hidden shift, some held out), LoRA, LoRA-FA and full fine-tuning of W itself, with the loss on the examples trained on, on the held-out ones, and on old examples, what the layer gave before any training, measured after it (retention).

P : Float

value · line 115

The row space of the frozen A: the projection onto it is P = A' (A A')^-1 A, through Matrix's inverse (ask FL3's workaround). A matrix's rows projected onto the subspace are M P; what is left, M - M P, is its remainder. The sizes (square root of the sum of squares) of the remainders of LoRA's update, LoRA-FA's and T itself: LoRA-FA's is 0 to rounding, its rows never left the subspace; LoRA's is not; T's is what LoRA-FA cannot reach.
