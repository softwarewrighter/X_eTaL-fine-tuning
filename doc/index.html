  • program demos/landscape/landscape.xtl: The landscape: the accounting of the ways to change a layer, each figure analytical (it follows from the method's definition, no training is run). For two layers, this course's 8 x 8 at rank 4 and one real layer, 4096 x 4096 at rank 16: for each family whose cost is a count, the numbers trained, the numbers held while training beyond the weights themselves (the gradient and Adam's two averages, where there are any), and the numbers stored afterward when the change is kept apart from W. Then the bytes of the layer itself at 16 bits and at 4 bits (QLoRA's base), and the adapter's share of the full count.

  • library work/xetal-ml/libs/Optim/src/Optim.xtl: Optim: optimizers as steps: gradient descent and Adam on a weight array and its averages
  • program demos/lora-hello/lora-hello.xtl: LoRA hello: what does a small adapter add to a layer whose weights do not change? The question behind it: how can a model learn something new without every one of its numbers being retrained (the cost) or overwritten (the forgetting)? LoRA's answer is to freeze the weights and train a small correction beside them. Here is one layer of that, shrunk to 4 rows and 4 columns so every number can be read: a frozen table W and an adapter of rank 1, with X_eTaL-ML's LoRA library. Nothing is trained here (the adapter's numbers are made up): this shows what an adapter is, where it sits, what it adds and what it costs. The layer is y = W x + s B (A x): W stays as it is, and only A (1 row of 4) and B (1 column of 4) would be trained.
  • program demos/lora-lab/lora-lab.xtl: LoRA lab: how an adapter's correction is worked out, number by number, and what its rank changes. A frozen table W of 8 rows and 8 columns, and an adapter of rank r beside it: A (r rows of 8) and B (8 rows of r). The layer is y = W x + s B (A x), written here as a pipeline of four stages so that every stage's numbers can be shown: the input x; the squeeze h = A x (r numbers); the spread u = B h (8 numbers); and the output y = W x + s u. The layer is the one of X_eTaL-ML's LoRA explorer (its W, A, B and x, made up by fixed ripples). Nothing is trained here.
  • library work/xetal-libraries/libs/Equations/src/Equations.xtl: Equations: sums as printed in papers, written in one macro call (e:e_q<, e:t_ex<, e:b_oth< in Equations.xtlm); here the valid 2-D cross-correlation of a CNN layer, as array code, which checks the macro.   • program demos/lora-training/lora-training.xtl: LoRA training: where the numbers of A and B come from. A frozen 8 x 8 layer W; examples (inputs with the outputs wanted) made by a hidden shift of it, W + T; an adapter that starts as LoRA does, B at 0 and A seeded. One step of training as a pipeline, stage by stage: the prediction, the error (which is the loss's gradient at the output), the gradients of A and B, the update; checked against the library's own step, W untouched. Then training by Adam with a record: the loss on the examples trained on and on examples held out, every few steps, and the adapter at each record, so a page can step back without training again.

  • library std/Svg.xtl: Svg: pictures as SVG text, written for you (a standard library, built into xetal). Import it with an alias of your choice: "v:" u_se< "Svg". An element is its SVG text; a picture is elements joined with c_at inside v:p_icture, which []S_HOW shows and the command line writes. Points are a 2-row matrix, x over y, as []P_ATH and Geometry3D's g:p_roject give them; y grows downward, as SVG's does.   • system macros std/System.xtlm: The system macros (lang-choices MC18, MC19, MC24), loaded before every file with no import and no alias: each is called unprefixed, as "c" i_f< "a; b". A macro is a function of the source text on each side of its call; the text it gives replaces the call. @ on a side means the macro takes nothing there (MC22). What only the compiler knows comes from hooks (MC20), usable only in a macro body.
  • program demos/lora-vs-fa/lora-vs-fa.xtl: LoRA beside LoRA-FA, in two experiments kept apart. Experiment A, approximating a known matrix: both fit a known rank-4 shift T from the same seeded A, B at 0; LoRA moves A and B, LoRA-FA moves B alone. The best any update of the rank can do (the Eckart-Young optimum, from T's singular values), and the floor LoRA-FA cannot pass: its update s B A has its rows in the row space of the frozen A, a subspace, so the closest it can come to T is T's projection onto that subspace. Each learned update is projected onto the row space and the remainder measured. Experiment B, learning from examples: the task of lesson 3 (examples made by the hidden shift, some held out), LoRA, LoRA-FA and full fine-tuning of W itself, with the loss on the examples trained on, on the held-out ones, and on old examples, what the layer gave before any training, measured after it (retention).

  • program demos/rank-and-scaling/rank-and-scaling.xtl: Rank and scaling: the adapter's rank swept from 1 to 8 on the learning task of lesson 3 (a frozen 8 x 8 layer, examples from a hidden shift of it, 8 trained on and 4 held out), under two rules for the strength s: LoRA's alpha / r (lo:s_caling) and rsLoRA's alpha / sqrt r (lo:s_calingRs). For each rank and rule, after the same number of Adam steps from the same kind of start: the numbers trained (analytical), the strength (analytical), the loss per example on the examples trained on and on the held-out ones (measured), and the size of the adapter's table s B A (measured: the square root of the sum of its squares).
  • program demos/welcome/welcome.xtl: Welcome: one small calculation on which a newcomer is shown how these lessons work (the page steps through it, pictures it, writes one of its sums out). It is the layer of the first lesson, LoRA hello: a frozen table W of 4 rows and 4 columns with an adapter of rank 1, written this time as a pipeline: the input x, then the squeeze h = A x, then the spread u = B h, then the output y = W x + s u. X_eTaL-ML's Flow runs the pipeline and can report every stage's value, which is what a page steps through.
