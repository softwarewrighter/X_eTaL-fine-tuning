programdemos/sinkhorn/sinkhorn.xtl

Sinkhorn-Knopp: a table whose rows and columns each sum to 1. mHC (Xie and others, arXiv:2512.24880) makes its carried-state mixing table from a positive 4 x 4 by dividing every row by its sum, then every column by its sum, twenty times over (its eq. 9, t_max = 20). Part 1: a positive 4 x 4 (the exponential of a table of logits: the paper's and the Python's start 2 I - 1, or a start set up as data), its row and column sums, and one round of the two divisions as a pipeline with every number. Part 2: the twenty rounds, the tables and their sums after every half round, how far the sums are from 1 after each round, and the count of rounds to within a millionth. Part 3: the three properties the paper states of the result (section 4.1), checked by the Check library: its largest singular value is at most 1 (a list multiplied by it does not grow; power iteration written out, ask FL2 of X_eTaL-libraries), the product of two such tables is one too, and it is a weighted mix of permutation tables (Birkhoff), found by a greedy search over the 24 orderings. The Sinkhorn loop is written out here (ask FM13 of X_eTaL-ML, docs/xetal-ML-asks.md).
