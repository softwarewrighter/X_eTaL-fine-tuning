Shared subspaces in X_eTaL-fine-tuning
Yes, shared subspaces deserve explicit coverage in X_eTaL-fine-tuning. They are more than a performance optimization, although some implementations are primarily intended to reduce memory consumption and trainable parameter counts.

The important distinction is between three ideas:

Shared parameter subspaces: Multiple layers, tasks, or adapters learn within a common low-dimensional space.

Shared update subspaces: Multiple fine-tuning operations reuse, combine, or constrain the directions in which model weights can change.

Shared search subspaces: Gradient-free methods explore a common, structured subset of weight space rather than independently perturbing millions or billions of parameters.

These ideas connect LoRA, VeRA, multi-task adaptation, continual learning, and newer derivative-free approaches such as evolutionary strategies.

For your educational demo, I would treat shared subspaces as a cross-cutting concept, not simply another LoRA variant. It can explain both why parameter-efficient fine-tuning works and how entirely different optimization methods can operate on the same reduced representation.

Related, but distinct

The most interesting paper for your immediate purposes is Shared LoRA Subspaces for almost Strict Continual Learning (February 2026).

Its Share approach maintains an evolving common subspace and integrates successive tasks. The authors report up to 100× fewer parameters and 281× memory savings relative to traditional LoRA in their evaluated settings, with comparable performance to jointly trained models. These are reported experimental results, not universal guarantees. 

arXiv

Two complementary directions are particularly important:

LoDA, published at ICML 2026, explicitly models general directions that should be shared and task-specific directions that should remain isolated. This is a more sophisticated view than simply sharing every direction. 

Proceedings of Machine Learning Research

SuLoRA, from ACL 2025, approaches the interference problem by allocating independent subspaces to different tasks. This gives you a useful contrast: sometimes sharing helps transfer learning, while separating helps prevent destructive interference. 

ACL Anthology

The Fisher-subspace and OPLoRA work further demonstrate that which directions you choose can matter as much as how many parameters you train. 

Proceedings of Machine Learning Research

4. Why this is more than an optimization
Consider your objective of teaching a coding model modern Rust practices: composition, delegation, pure functions, modularity, and low cyclomatic complexity.

Suppose you fine-tune sequentially:

Task A: Prefer composition over inheritance-like designs.

Task B: Produce small, pure functions.

Task C: Reduce cyclomatic complexity.

Task D: Improve runtime performance.

These tasks are related, but not identical.

With independent LoRA adapters, you may end up with four adaptations that partly duplicate the same knowledge. Combining them can introduce interference.

A shared-subspace approach raises a different question:

Can the model learn reusable directions representing good software-engineering practices, then refine those directions as new feedback arrives?

That is a continual-learning problem, not merely parameter compression.

Conceptual example: overlap between task subspaces


The potential benefits include better knowledge transfer, fewer adapters, lower storage costs, and less catastrophic forgetting.

The risks are equally important. A shared basis can become a bottleneck, unrelated tasks may interfere, and updating the basis can change the behavior of previously learned tasks. A fixed basis avoids some instability but can prevent learning genuinely new directions.

Consequently, I would not teach shared subspaces as inherently superior to independent adapters.

I would introduce shared subspaces as a foundational bridge between LoRA variations, continual learning, and gradient-free weight-space optimization.

The most valuable first experiment is not reproducing a complex 2026 paper. It is demonstrating, with real X_eTaL calculations, that sharing a basis between related tasks can improve efficiency or transfer, while sharing between unrelated tasks can introduce interference.

That gives beginners a useful intuition and establishes the mathematical foundation for Share, LoDA, and more advanced approaches later.

One final distinction matters for your project: shared subspaces are a representation strategy, not an optimizer. They can be paired with backpropagation, derivative-free optimization, or certain direct-editing methods. That makes them a particularly useful organizing concept for X_eTaL-fine-tuning.
